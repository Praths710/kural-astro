Latest discovery

Neutron stars behind a mysterious X-ray flash

Since 2024 the Einstein Probe telescope has caught brief X-ray flashes with no known source. One of them, EP250704a, has now been traced to its cause: two neutron stars colliding about 6 billion light years away. A half-second gamma-ray burst came first, then an X-ray flash lasting almost ten minutes, the longest ever seen from such a merger. The collision most likely left behind a magnetar, a fast-spinning neutron star with an enormous magnetic field.
